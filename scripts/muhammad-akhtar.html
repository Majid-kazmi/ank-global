RCM Assuming the service is subject to Saudi VAT: Service = SAR 100,000 RCM VAT @ 15% = SAR 15,000 Output VAT = SAR 15,000 Potential input VAT = SAR 15,000 So, if fully recoverable, the net VAT effect can be zero. 2. WHT Consulting/technical services may also be subject to WHT depending on the applicable rules and circumstances. ZATCA's current general WHT guidance lists 5% for technical and consulting services, with specific rules that can affect the rate, including payments to a head office or related company. For a SAR 100,000 payment at 5%: Gross payment = SAR 100,000 WHT = SAR 5,000 Amount paid to foreign supplier = SAR 95,000 SAR 5,000 remitted to ZATCA 🔑 The easiest way to remember RCM = VAT mechanism > Foreign supplier → Saudi customer self-accounts for VAT. WHT = withholding mechanism > Saudi customer → deducts income tax from payment to foreign supplier → pays it to ZATCA. And importantly, the same transaction can potentially trigger both RCM and WHT. They are not alternatives to each other. ZATCA confirms that WHT applies to relevant Saudi-source payments to non-residents, and the WHT return is generally filed by the 10th day of the following month.

    IFRS 15 – Revenue from Contracts with Customers. During my professional-level classes, we studied several accounting standards, but the one I found most interesting was IFRS 15. IFRS 15 provides a clear framework for how and when revenue should be recognized. It is built around a 5-step model, and if any of these steps is missing, IFRS 15 does not apply. Let’s break down what the standard says 👇 IFRS 15 – Revenue from Contracts with Customers. IFRS 15 establishes a single, comprehensive framework for revenue recognition, replacing multiple industry-specific standards. Its objective is to recognize revenue in a way that faithfully depicts the transfer of control of goods or services to customers, at an amount the entity expects to be entitled to. 🔹 The 5-Step Revenue Recognition Model 1. Identify the contract A contract exists when it has commercial substance, is approved by the parties, has clearly defined rights and payment terms, and collectability is probable. 2. Identify performance obligations Performance obligations are distinct goods or services (or a series of services) promised in the contract. A good or service is distinct if the customer can benefit from it on its own and it is separately identifiable. 3. Determine the transaction price The transaction price is the amount of consideration expected, adjusted for: * Variable consideration (bonuses, penalties, discounts) * Constraints on variable consideration * Significant financing components * Non-cash consideration * Consideration payable to customers 4. Allocate the transaction price The transaction price is allocated to each performance obligation based on relative standalone selling price, using estimation techniques when observable prices are not available. 5. Recognize revenue Revenue is recognized when or as performance obligations are satisfied: Over time (e.g., long-term services or construction contracts) At a point in time (e.g., delivery of goods) 🔹 Key Areas Requiring Judgment * Over-time vs. point-in-time recognition * Principal vs. agent determination * Contract modifications * Warranties (assurance vs. service) * Licensing arrangements * Costs to obtain or fulfill contracts (capitalization and amortization) 🔹 Why IFRS 15 Matters * Enhances consistency and comparability across entities * Aligns revenue recognition with economic substance, not just invoicing * Increases focus on judgment, estimates, disclosures, and controls * Can significantly impact reported revenue patterns, KPIs, and systems A strong understanding of IFRS 15 is essential for accurate financial reporting and effective contract structuring in today’s complex business environment. 📊 #IFRS #IFRS15 #RevenueRecognition #Accounting #Finance
