    In a 1969 photo of Al-Masmak Palace in Riyadh, a signboard—likely belonging to a Pakistani doctor—stands out, reminding us of an era when most clinics in the Kingdom relied on physicians from Pakistan. They came driven by service, treating patients in remote villages and towns, speaking Arabic with a distinctive accent, and carrying bags full of hope. They were a human bridge between two nations, before the local healthcare system evolved. That signboard is not just an advertisement, but a testament to sacrifice and medical expertise that helped build the health of an entire generation. 🇸🇦❤️🇵🇰 During the 1960s, healthcare in Saudi Arabia was in its formative stage, especially in Riyadh, where government efforts focused on establishing basic dispensaries and hospitals to address the shortage of medical staff. There were no widespread official or community-based “Pakistani clinics” in Riyadh as seen today; instead, Pakistani doctors primarily worked in government or military facilities like Ministry of Health dispensaries or the National Guard Hospital (officially founded in 1983, but with roots in the 1960s). However, as part of international cooperation, they began arriving and contributed to setting up individual or small private clinics in urban areas like Riyadh, often under short-term contracts (two years) to support primary care in remote or developing regions. Notable Documented Contributions of Pakistani Doctors in Saudi Arabia • Dr. Nazeer Ahmed Khan: Arrived in Riyadh in 1963; one of the earliest Pakistani physicians there. Worked in primary government clinics, then established a small private clinic in Riyadh, contributing to care for decades. Died in 2020 at age 95, remembered as a symbol of Pakistani contribution to Saudi healthcare renaissance. • Dr. Farooq Rashid: Moved to Riyadh in the mid-1960s (around 1965); started in military or civilian clinics, later helped develop private practices. Returned to Pakistan in the 1980s but is regarded as a medical bridge between the two countries. • Dr. Noor Muhammad Khan Clinic. Arrived from Pakistan in 1962; served as director of Hafr Al-Batin government dispensary, then founded the first private clinic in the 1960s, which grew into a full hospital worth 50 million SAR. Granted Saudi citizenship in the 1970s; passed away in 2024 as an icon of medical pioneering. Pakistan lives in our hearts—doctors who healed deserts, built hope with hands, spoke love in Arabic. From 1960s clinics to today’s hospitals, you are our eternal brothers. 🇸🇦🤝🇵🇰
