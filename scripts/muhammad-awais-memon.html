    I have conducted AI sessions for professionals, builders, and corporate teams. But teaching AI to college students was a different kind of experience altogether. I got an opportunity to be a guest lecturer for MBA students on AI, and it was genuinely a different experience. Coming from a non-technical background, I learned AI the hard way. Through experiments, failures, countless hours of figuring things out, and eventually building real products. That journey changed my life. Today, I build AI products and work as an Enterprise AI Architect, helping companies design and implement AI solutions to solve real business problems. And somewhere along this journey, I started sharing my experiences with others. It began with non-tech professionals, then AI builder communities, followed by corporate AI sessions. And now, I am getting the opportunity to bring those experiences into college classrooms. What made teaching college students special was seeing the curiosity and their willingness to explore AI. I am genuinely happy that colleges are recognizing the importance of exposing students to AI while they're still studying. But there is something I strongly believe about teaching AI. I don't tell people that AI is a mandatory skill they must learn to survive. You cannot force someone to embrace technology. Instead, I show them what's possible. How AI can help them solve problems, turn their ideas into products, create opportunities, or simply do things they never thought they could. What they do with AI is entirely their choice. My role is to help them see the possibilities. And that's why I enjoy these sessions, not sharing AI just from presentations or theory. I am sharing what I experience while building products, experimenting with technology, and implementing AI solutions for businesses. Including the things that didn't work. Because I know what its like to start from zero without a technical background. Looking back, from non-tech professionals to builder communities, corporate teams, and now college students, the audience keeps changing, but the purpose remains the same. To democratize AI AI has empowered me to build things I once wouldn't have imagined possible. And I want more people to experience that same sense of possibility. Not everyone needs to become an AI engineer or an AI expert. But everyone deserves the opportunity to understand what AI can make possible for them. And if my journey can help someone else take that first step, I will continue sharing it to as many people as possible.

    Our AI Community's September meetup was scheduled to close at 2 but People were still talking at 3 Hosting this month's Outskill UAE chapter meetup meant I heard all the sessions twice, once as an attendee and once while discussing them prior to the event. 30 of us at @Mena plaza hotel MENA Hotels and Resorts UAE, Four people presented and Not one of them spent a minute arguing about which model is best. Divyesh Ardeshana, Founder at Savvy Data Cloud Consulting, who presented one of his own products at our July meet and currently has 7 apps running in production, opened with From Prompt to Production. He built a mini CRM while teaching the method behind it. Unglamorous and immediately usable. Make the model write your rules file. Work one feature at a time. Make the agent document its own API, endpoints and tables as it goes, so in six months you still know why something is the way it is. On tool FOMO he was blunt: pick 1 and drive it like a car, without looking behind you. On free tiers he was blunter - Pay the 20$. Shameer Thaha, presented FinalTake.ai. A founder of Infinite Barakah Ventures, runs a lighting business by day and has built products for both B2C and B2B. He put a real estate brochure in one end and got a finished video out the other, with an AI crew arguing about it in between: a director of photography, a script supervisor checking continuity, a verifier that catches a bad shot and re-renders it. His rule from the debugging: when an error makes no sense, test your payload against the smallest request that works. His rule on the product: generation is cheap, the direction is the actual product. Cody S. presented in public for the first time, which is worth pausing on. 8 to 10 months building Vedaxi: intent-based computing on WebMCP and A2A, where you state a goal in plain language and the system hands you the evidence trail of how it got there. Syed Hasan, a renowned Alumni of Outskill and a big mentor to many of us, closed with 45 minutes of open Q&A. He ships products in two to three hours, runs his own vetted skills marketplace, and won an OpenAI hackathon that started with 10,000 people and ended with 5 winners. His explanation was flat. Building is easy now. He won on his construction audit background, the Dubai Metro, the Expo, Atlantis. Then he mentioned he is not a coder. Thank you to Divyesh, Shameer, Cody and Syed bhai, all who prepared & presented for nothing in return. And to everyone attended and asked real questions. What I took home: the moat is what you knew before you ever opened a terminal.
